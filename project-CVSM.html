Cartesian Variable Stiffness Mechanism

August 2023 - December 2025

Motivation & Objectives

In industrial manufacturing, robots excel at pick-and-place tasks and handling large heavy loads, however, these applications also cause exacerbated wear due to the high inertial loads. To mitigate this, we introduce the design of a novel cartesian variable stiffness mechanism placed between the robot arm and its end effector aimed to reduce these forces and provide extended capabilities. In this work, we developed several concepts for tuning stiffness and present a working prototype with a demonstrated use case in a pick-place scenario.

Goals

  • Investigate possible VSM designs to compare their reachable stiffnesses and which features offer increased compactness and stiffness resolution
  • Analytically characterize each concept's stiffness profile and support with FEA and physical testing
  • Design an initial prototype to inform future design decisions
  • If possible, simulate a pick-and-place scenario and test the device's efficacy

Outcomes

  • Analytically derived several stiffness models based on different VSM concepts
  • Verified stiffness behavior using Finite Element Analysis and performed physical testing
  • Built a working prototype with independently tunable stiffnesses for both the XY and Z stages
  • Created an open loop control program to actuate between different levels of stiffness
  • Commissioned a UR5 robot out of the box and mounted the CVSM for facilitated testing
  • Successfully demonstrated its ability to aid in pick-and-place/assembly operations and protect the robot from potential crashes
